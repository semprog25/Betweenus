SECTION 1
Heading:
Every Office Has One

Block:
Paragraph

Content:
Every workplace has that person.

They know who is leaving.

They know who is annoyed with management.

They know who is dating someone.

They know why Sarah suddenly started working from home on Fridays.

And somehow, they knew about the meeting before the meeting was even scheduled.

You don't know how they know.

You don't even want to know.

But somehow, you always end up hearing it.

SECTION 2
Heading:
The Mysterious Meeting

Block:
Paragraph

Content:
Nothing creates office gossip faster than a mysterious meeting.

Someone gets invited to:

“A quick chat.”

No agenda.

No explanation.

Just a calendar invitation.

Immediately, everyone becomes an amateur detective.

“Do you think it's about…”

“Did you notice…”

“Maybe they're…”

And within twenty minutes, three completely different theories exist.

Block:
Callout

Content:
A meeting with no agenda is basically an invitation for everyone's imagination.

SECTION 3
Heading:
The Lunchroom News Network

Block:
Paragraph

Content:
Office gossip rarely happens in the middle of a meeting.

It happens in corridors.

At the coffee machine.

In the elevator.

During lunch.

In private messages that begin with:

“Are you hearing this too?”

Someone mentions something small.

Another person adds a detail.

Someone else says, “Actually, I heard…”

And suddenly you've got a story.

Whether the story is true is sometimes a completely different question.

SECTION 4
Heading:
When Gossip Gets Too Far

Block:
Paragraph

Content:
There's harmless office gossip.

Then there's gossip that can actually hurt someone.

Speculating about someone's private life, spreading rumours about their job, making assumptions about relationships or sharing confidential information can turn workplace chatter into something much more serious.

The best gossip is entertaining.

The worst gossip follows someone home.

Block:
Tip

Content:
Before passing on workplace gossip, ask:

Is this funny, or could this actually hurt someone?

That one question can save a lot of unnecessary drama.

SECTION 5
Heading:
And Yet… We Still Listen

Block:
Paragraph

Content:
Here's the uncomfortable truth.

Even when we know office gossip can get messy, most of us still listen when someone says:

“Okay, you didn't hear this from me…”

Because curiosity is powerful.

We want to know what's happening around us.

We want to understand the strange tension in the room.

We want to know why two colleagues suddenly stopped talking.

And sometimes, yes, we simply want the tea.

Block:
Callout

Content:
Just remember: hearing the gossip doesn't mean you have to become part of it.

Heading:
Every Workplace Has Stories

Block:
Paragraph

Content:
Every workplace has stories.

Some are true.

Some are exaggerated.

Some started because someone misunderstood an email.

And some will probably never be explained.

The smartest person in the office isn't necessarily the person who knows every secret.

Sometimes it's the person who knows when not to repeat one.
