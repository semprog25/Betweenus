SECTION 1
Heading:
We Don't Gossip… We “Share Information”

Block:
Paragraph

Content:
Nobody likes to admit they enjoy gossip.

Ask someone if they gossip and they'll probably say, “Not really. I just don't like talking about people.”

And then, five minutes later:

“Okay, but you didn't hear this from me…”

There is something strangely universal about gossip. It can happen at work, at school, in a family WhatsApp group, at a party, over coffee or in the back seat of a car. Sometimes it's completely harmless. Sometimes it's hilarious. And sometimes it is information that probably should never have left the room where it started.

Block:
Callout

Content:
“You didn't hear this from me” is probably one of the most dangerous sentences in the English language.

SECTION 2
Heading:
Why Is It So Hard to Resist?

Block:
Paragraph

Content:
Gossip gives us information that feels exclusive.

Knowing something other people don't know creates a tiny feeling of importance. Suddenly you're not just standing around talking. You're holding information.

And the more surprising the information is, the harder it becomes not to pass it on.

Someone got fired.

Someone is secretly dating someone.

Two best friends aren't speaking.

Someone posted something online that they definitely didn't mean everyone to see.

Suddenly the conversation has a plot.

Block:
Paragraph

Content:
The funny thing is that gossip doesn't even have to be negative. Sometimes people gossip about good news, unexpected relationships, promotions, weddings, breakups or someone's bizarre new hobby.

The common factor is simple:

It's interesting.

SECTION 3
Heading:
When Gossip Becomes a Problem

Block:
Paragraph

Content:
There is a line between sharing a funny story and spreading something that can genuinely hurt someone.

The problem is that the line isn't always obvious.

A story can start as:

“Did you hear what happened?”

Then become:

“Apparently…”

Then:

“I heard that…”

And eventually:

“Everyone knows.”

Except nobody actually knows whether it's true.

Block:
Tip

Content:
Before passing something on, ask yourself:

Would I say this if the person were standing right next to me?

If the answer is definitely no, maybe the story belongs in your head rather than the group chat.

SECTION 4
Heading:
But Let's Be Honest…

Block:
Paragraph

Content:
Gossip is not disappearing.

People are naturally curious about other people. We want to understand what is happening around us, who likes whom, what happened after the party and why someone suddenly stopped following someone else.

The trick isn't pretending we don't enjoy it.

It's knowing when a funny story is just a funny story—and when you're holding someone's private life in your hands.

Block:
Callout

Content:
The best gossip is the kind that makes you laugh without ruining someone's life.

Block:
Paragraph

Content:
So yes, we probably all gossip.

Some people are just better at pretending they don't.

And if you have ever received a message beginning with “Don't tell anyone, but…”, you already know exactly what we're talking about.
